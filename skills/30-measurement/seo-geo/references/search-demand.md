# Map real search demand to pages

Use when choosing queries, diagnosing weak organic coverage or deciding whether
two pages serve the same need. Work from customer questions, supplied research,
GSC observations and, when relevant, Ads search terms or keyword planning.
Keep the original wording, date, locale and source before grouping by meaning.

Build a useful map: query or question, buyer task, segment/market, observed
evidence, current landing page, business outcome evidence and proposed action.
Classify discovery, comparison, price/eligibility, purchase and implementation
needs only where the wording or observed results support the classification.
One phrase can carry several intents; inspect the real result pages and customer
context before merging it with another topic.

GSC clicks and impressions describe observed coverage, not total market demand.
Keyword Planner forecasts and Ads competition describe advertising estimates,
not organic difficulty. Missing query rows can reflect reporting limits. Paid
conversion data may be delayed or optimize a different event from a qualified
customer. Use Measurement and Ads to resolve those definitions before ranking
opportunities. Do not join aggregate queries to individual orders without an
actual supported identity link.

## Resolve coverage problems

- For a relevant query landing on an unhelpful page, compare its intent with the
  page promise, available product proof and actual user path. Improve the owning
  page or propose a more suitable destination; preserve the previous evidence.
- For similar pages, inspect their intent, index/canonical evidence, incoming
  links and existing outcomes. Assign a primary page for planning; only merge or
  redirect after the affected traffic and authorization are understood. Two
  ranking pages are not automatically a defect.
- For an uncovered need, check whether the product genuinely serves it and
  whether an existing page can answer it. New pages need distinct value; a list
  of keyword permutations is not a content plan.

Prioritize by expected qualified value, evidence strength, current page leverage
and effort. Explain the variables deciding the order rather than impose a fixed
score or search-volume floor. Deliver a short queue with the owning page,
proposed change, source facts and the observation that would confirm or reverse
the decision. Use [content planning](content-planning.md) for the actual brief
and [paid/organic planning](paid-organic.md) for a budget-related decision.
