# Street Blind Test Challenge

**The participant experiences unidentified samples, describes differences, and commits to a preference before learning the brands.** A blindfold is one method; screened or neutral containers also work. The audience may see identities that the participant cannot.

## Preserve

`Conceal identity → experience comparable options → describe differences → commit to a choice → reveal → relevant product payoff`

- Make the concealment and comparison understandable on screen. Keep sample codes, positions, and identities consistent through use and reveal.
- Show the experience before the verdict: bite or sip, pause, expression, then a specific observation. The host asks and follows up; the participant supplies the preference.
- Obtain a clear choice before revealing its identity to the participant. Connect the reveal to a relevant, verified product fact and a short close.
- Keep the causal sequence even when the edit opens with a reaction or intercuts several participants. A montage need not replay the whole test for every person.

## Adapt

Change the samples, count, concealment method, sensory dimensions, dialogue, verified facts, and close. Use this format when the relevant difference can be experienced in the scene; a taste preference cannot establish a delayed health or performance outcome.

Retain brief questions, specific everyday comparisons, uncertain reactions, and host follow-ups rather than giving everyone a polished sales pitch.

Follow the reference's observed cuts and holds.

Use the shared three-part prompt. Its filming and performance directions specify **who can see the brands, the sample mapping, and the choice-before-reveal sequence**; its complete dialogue supplies the new product experience. Keep generation and long-video handling in the main skill.
