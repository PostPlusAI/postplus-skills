# Playful Street Challenge

A street-interview subtype: **a simple task creates suspense; participation, interaction, and a visible result give the product a natural role.** Timed treasure hunts, guessing tasks, and games can fit. Follow the reference's actual mechanism.

## Preserve the Mechanism

`Understandable task → acceptance → visible action and feedback → clear result → product payoff and close`

- Establish what to do, what counts as completion, and why viewers should keep watching. Preserve a timer, score, or prize when it drives the reference; none is mandatory for every challenge.
- The participant performs the task. The host initiates, asks, reacts, and gives feedback. Alternate conversation, action, and expressions; dialogue can continue over action shots.
- The product may be the target, the tool, or the reward. Choose the roles the concept needs. Product dialogue belongs in natural narrative gaps and relates to the action.
- Preserve the task and emotional progression. Adapt product facts, props, cast, and necessary dialogue. Ground reward terms in the user's materials.

Game interactions follow the same principle: actions and results explain the rules; participant reactions demonstrate the fun. When concealed brands and experience-based preference drive the story, use the main skill's Street Blind Test Challenge structure instead.

Use the main skill's three-part prompt: **asset bindings, filming and performance, complete dialogue**. Add the relevant task, role actions, and suspense; do not create a separate generation workflow.
