# Problem-to-Replacement Interview

**A concrete complaint earns the product introduction; a visible demonstration answers that complaint.** The brand may be visible throughout. This is a street-interview subtype, not a blind test.

## Preserve

`Recognizable usage context → specific frustration → relevant product introduction → hands-on demonstration → participant response → short close`

- Establish why this person would use the product. Elicit a specific inconvenience in their words before explaining the solution.
- Pair the complaint with a corresponding action or detail the viewer can inspect. A list of features without a connection to the problem loses the mechanism.
- Keep the roles clear: the host asks and guides the demonstration; the participant describes the need, handles or inspects the product, and responds to what changed.
- Let questions, handling, and reactions carry the explanation. Product commentary can continue over close-ups; return to the conversation after each useful detail.

## Adapt

Replace the usage context, complaint, demonstration, and evaluation together. Choose a problem the new product can actually address and an action that makes the relevant benefit visible within the scene. Do not inherit the source's feature list or imply that briefly handling a product proves long-term results.

Retain short questions, interruptions where useful, specific complaints, curiosity during handling, and reactions tied to a detail. Give participants distinct responses rather than identical endorsements.

Use question/answer two-shots, faces, hands, the relevant product detail, and the resulting reaction according to the reference's observed cuts and holds. Match the supplied reference's framing and transitions; decorative treatments can change without removing the complaint-to-demonstration link.

Use the shared three-part prompt. Its filming and performance directions supply the **complaint-to-demonstration connection and role actions**; its complete dialogue reflects the new product. Keep generation and long-video handling in the main skill.
