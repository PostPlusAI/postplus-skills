# Strategy and review

Use this when the user asks what to publish, where, how often, or why a recent
post did or did not work. A schedule is an experiment matched to the team's
capacity, not a universal frequency target.

## Choose a defensible content direction

1. Name the business or community outcome: qualified visit, answer to a
   recurring question, useful discussion, retention, or another observable
   action. Separate it from a vanity metric such as raw likes.
2. Describe the reader's situation and evidence of where that reader already
   talks or searches. Choose a platform for a real audience and suitable
   format, not age stereotypes or unverified algorithm claims.
3. Choose a few repeatable questions the brand can answer credibly. For each,
   record source material, a helpful angle, format, and the action a reader can
   take. Balance teaching, proof, experience, and promotion according to the
   user's objective; do not impose a fixed percentage split.
4. Check the team's source material and production capacity. Prefer one
   sustainable sequence over a calendar filled with invented topics. Reuse a
   long-form asset only when its claims and rights are clear.

Plan each post as `reader question → useful answer → supporting example or
evidence → one next action`. For an experiment, state the change being tested
(topic, opening, format, audience, or distribution), the comparison post or
period, and the earliest sensible review point. Avoid changing all variables
at once when the goal is learning.

## Diagnose before recommending more volume

For the user's own posts, read [channel execution](channel-execution.md) and
the relevant platform file. Fetch the exact post or account and the metric the
tool actually exposes. Check the publication time and timezone, distribution
scope, audience, completeness, any paid boost, and whether the posts had
comparable goals. Contrast exposure with the subsequent response: low reach
asks a distribution/eligibility question; reach with little engagement asks a
message/format question; engaged readers without desired action ask a journey
question. These are hypotheses, not diagnoses without further evidence.

For each recommendation report the observed numbers and their source, an
alternative explanation, one low-cost next test, and what result would change
the recommendation. Do not claim that a platform metric equals qualified
leads, sales, or attributed revenue. Where a metric is unavailable, name that
gap instead of substituting a public like count.
